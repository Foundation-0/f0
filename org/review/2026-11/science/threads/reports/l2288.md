15 November 2026.

**Result: all 2,288 realizations are real, and that is certified.** [F] At certified squared edge lengths, all 2,288 complex realizations of L2288 (GKT code 18120782205838348) are real. All 1,144 mirror pairs have boxes. Each box holds exactly one real solution with a nonsingular Jacobian, and the boxes are pairwise disjoint.
- **Lengths:** in `lengths_2288.json`. Squared lengths are 6-decimal rationals, scale λ(3,9)=1, all lengths within 0.8% of 1.
- **Certificate:** `cert_2288_c_dec6.json`. It passes two checkers I wrote separately: `certify.py` (exact rationals, Newton-operator bounds) and `certify_arb.py` (interval Krawczyk in arb ball arithmetic). Both also pass under `python -O`.
- **Corruption tests:** a shifted centre, a changed length, a duplicate box, a moved pin and a changed edge are each rejected. The unchanged control passes.
- **Upper bound:** Capco et al. (arXiv:1701.05500, table `max_laman_numbers`) give 2288 as the largest complex count for any 11-vertex Laman graph. So r₂(L2288) = 2288 [F]. r₂(11) = 2288 [I] also holds, but it rests on their computer result, which I did not recheck.

**How the repo got from 868 to 880.**
- The solutions are written in isotropic coordinates with a pinned triangle. A linear program (LP) works on all 440 half-solutions at once, inside a box trust region in the squared lengths.
- The objective is the gradient of the squared imaginary part of the non-real pairs. Constraints keep the 90 closest real pairs from moving more than a fraction closer, so no real pair collides at the opposite fold.
- A move goes along a complex detour arc, so a pair crossing its wall splits cleanly into two real roots. A step is accepted only if the real count rises or the objective falls.
- Two gaps: the code uses 0.25 where the note says 0.30. `lp_push_880.py` also loads `fast_climb_best.npz`, and no script in the repo makes that file, so the repo's push cannot be rerun from committed code.

**Solver.**
- `lam.py` and `monodromy.py` use parameters that are polynomial in the pinned vertex, so there is no square-root branch cut, and an adaptive tracker vectorised over all paths.
- A check on L880 gives 440 solutions, and 868 real at Bartzos's lengths, matching the published figure.
- For L2288 I found 1,144 solutions in about 40 s, and they saturated (three more loops added nothing).
- A simple trace test is not valid in this parametrisation: it fails even on the complete L880 set. So completeness rests on the agreement with 2,288 and on requiring 1,144 distinct solutions at every real point.

- **Henneberg seeding does not apply.** L2288 has minimum degree 3, and none of its 16 Henneberg II reductions is L880 (`graph_check.out`). The 1,760 figure belongs to a different 11-vertex graph.
- **Wide-shell start:** 1,176 → 2,144.
- **Walls:** at 2,280, two pairs stayed deep. In them, vertices {4,5,6,7,8,11} sit as a real-shaped block shifted by a complex translation.
  - Driving those two pairs real first, at the cost of dropping to 2,120, then climbing back without letting any pair get deeper, reached 2,288.
  - One stall came from the tracker's round-off floor rejecting badly conditioned paths. I relaxed it.
  - Rescaling the perturbation and pushing again improved the smallest singular value from 1.6e-8 to 7.3e-5.

**Asymptotic bound.** I checked the formula against the arXiv:1811.12800 TeX source.
- Gluing copies of G on a triangle (r₂ = 2) gives r₂(n) ≥ 2^((n−3) mod 8) · 2 · (N/2)^⌊(n−3)/8⌋.
- With N = 2288 the growth rate is 1144^(1/8) ≈ 2.4116^n. The published value is 430^(1/7) ≈ 2.378^n. Their abstract says 2.3811, which does not match their own corollary.
- At 1,760 there is no gain (2.334). It takes N ≥ 2,046 to beat 2.378, and N ≥ 2,100 to beat 440^(1/7) ≈ 2.3858, the rate if all 880 of L880's realizations are real.
