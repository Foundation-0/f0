# Thread: the 11-vertex graph, all 2,288 realizations real

Follow-up to the science pass, 15 November 2026. Pinned head `rigid-realizations@298926c`. Marks: **[F]** fact, **[I]** inference.

## Question

The repository made all 880 realizations of the largest 10-vertex rigid graph real. Its notes name the next target: the 11-vertex graph with the most complex realizations, 2,288 (integer code 18120782205838348). The notes report 1,582 real from exploratory runs, with no driver or data. Can the same push reach more, and can the result be certified?

## Result

[F] There are edge lengths for this graph at which at least 2,288 distinct non-singular real realizations exist. Verdict: **VERIFIED**. That these are all the complex realizations uses the generic complex count of 2,288 and a continuation argument that is cited, not computed here (see Not verified).

- [F] The certificate `checks/l2288/cert_2288_c_dec6.json` gives 19 exact squared lengths, a pinned triangle and 1,144 boxes. Each box is proved to hold exactly one real solution, the boxes are disjoint, and mirror images double the count to 2,288.
- [F] Three programs pass it. Two are exact-rational programs that implement the same proof with separately written code: `certify.py`, written with the search, and `l2288_independent.py`, written for this review. They share the proof template and the constants, so a mistake there would defeat both. The third, `certify_arb.py`, uses ball arithmetic, a different method. The worst contraction bound of `l2288_independent.py` is 3.7e-6 against the limit of 1.
- [F] `l2288_independent.py` rejects a changed length, a moved centre, a duplicated box, a moved pin and a replaced edge, also under `python3 -O`.
- [F] The graph in the certificate is the graph with that integer code, and its count of complex realizations is 2,288 by three implementations of one published recursion (see `rigid-realizations.md`).

All 19 lengths are within 0.8% of 1. The squared lengths are fractions with six decimal places.

## What it means

1. [F] For this graph at least 2,288 realizations are real. [I] Given the generic complex count and the continuation argument, that equals the number of complex ones and no larger count is possible for it.
2. [I] The published table of Capco and co-authors gives 2,288 as the largest complex count of any rigid graph on 11 vertices. If that table is right, 2,288 is the most real realizations any minimally rigid graph on 11 vertices can have. The table was not rechecked here.
3. [F] This step needs only the lower bound of 2,288. Gluing copies of the graph along a triangle gives at least 2 x 1144^k realizations on 3 + 8k vertices. The growth per vertex is 1144^(1/8) = 2.4116.

| Source | Graph | Real realizations | Growth per vertex |
|---|---|---|---|
| Bartzos and co-authors, published and verified | 10 vertices | 860 | 2.3780 |
| this organisation, verified | 10 vertices | 880 | 2.3858 |
| this thread, verified | 11 vertices | 2,288 | 2.4116 |

[F] A survey from February 2025 (Grasegger, arXiv:2502.04736) still quotes about 2.378 as the bound for real realizations. Two web searches and three paper sources found no report of 2,288 real, or of any bound above 2.378. This is "no earlier report found".

## How the search went

[F] The solver finds all 1,144 solutions at a generic complex point in about 40 seconds and follows them to real lengths. A linear program then moves the lengths so that pairs of complex solutions approach the real axis and split into two real ones, while keeping existing real pairs from colliding.

| Minutes from start | Real realizations | |
|---|---|---|
| 10 | 1,532 | near-unit lengths |
| 16 | 1,932 | certified |
| 23 | 2,180 | certified |
| 30 | 2,280 | certified |
| 51 | 2,288 | certified |

[F] At 2,280 two pairs stayed far from real. They were made real first, which cost 160 others, and the push then climbed back to 2,288 without letting any pair move away again.

[F] The 1,760 quoted in the repository's notes for 11 vertices belongs to a different graph. This graph has no vertex of degree two, and none of its 16 reductions is the 10-vertex graph.

## The 12-vertex graph does not raise the bound

The same pipeline was run on the 12-vertex graph with the most complex realizations, 6,180 (integer code 1514126886600097825).

- [F] The best point found has 3,904 real realizations. Three programs accept its certificate (`checks/l2288/v12/cert12_best.json`): two written with the search, and an edge-frame version of the reviewer's program, which follows the same proof template (`checks/l2288/edge_frame_independent.py`). 
- [F] Their outputs, run again under `python3 -O` after the team review found none saved, are `certify12_best.out`, `certify12_arb_best.out` and `edge_frame_independent_best.out` in `checks/l2288/v12/`. The reviewer's triangle-frame program does not apply to this graph, and its refusal is kept as `triangle_frame_checker_not_applicable.out`. The graph has no triangle, so one edge is pinned and mirror images are counted as separate boxes.
- [F] The graph contains no triangle and no rigid proper subgraph on three or more vertices. Copies can only be glued along an edge, which adds 10 vertices per copy, so N real realizations give growth of N^(1/10).
- [F] Even all 6,180 real would give 6180^(1/10) = 2.394, below the 2.4116 above. This graph cannot raise the bound however many of its realizations are real.
- [I] Adding one vertex of degree two to the 11-vertex graph doubles its count, so 12 vertices already have a graph with 4,576 real realizations.
- [F] Near equal lengths, 588 of the 6,180 solutions run off to infinity and can never be real there. That caps the near-unit method at about 5,592 for this graph.

An earlier version of this note said that all 6,180 real would give 2.442 per vertex. That used the triangle formula, which does not apply to this graph.

## Not verified

- The search path cannot be replayed step for step, because one solver setting was changed during the run. The certificate is the evidence, and it stands alone.
- That 2,288 is all of the complex realizations at the certified lengths. The certificate proves at least 2,288. The upper bound uses the generic count 2,288 together with the Morgan and Sommese continuation theorem, which is cited and not computed here. Extra degenerate or isolated solutions at these exact lengths are not excluded by a program.
- The solver's count of 1,144 was not confirmed by a separate completeness test. The complex count rests on the recursion instead.
- The published table behind point 2.
