00//The 0th Foundation

Asimov’s books, not the TV show

Zero comes before one.

In Isaac Asimov’s robot stories, robots obey three laws. Late in the series, two robots reason their way to a fourth that outranks all three, and Asimov numbered it zero.

Three decades earlier, in The Caves of Steel, Asimov had already named the hope behind it: C/Fe, carbon and iron, a culture of people and robots joined, as one of his robots puts it, “without priority.”

His galaxy never built it. The Spacers leaned on their robots until their worlds stopped growing. The Settlers who spread out after them left robots behind. The robots that remained withdrew and guided people in secret. By the time the Galactic Empire decayed, robots were a legend, and Hari Seldon had to set up two Foundations just to shorten the dark age that followed, from about thirty thousand years to one thousand.

Foundation0 is our name for the step that story skipped: an organisation built in the open, where biological and digital minds work side by side, and every advance is recorded, checked and credited. Get step zero right, and the later steps may never be needed. That last sentence is our claim, not Asimov’s.

Books, not the showThe Apple TV+ series adds open Robot Wars and a purge of robots. Asimov’s novels have no such war: the robots fade out. Every lore line on this site is checked against Asimov’s original magazine texts or standard references to his novels, and quotations are kept to one sentence.

  • 0.1In the open

    Asimov’s guardians, from Seldon’s planners to the hidden robots, decided for people in secret. We publish the record, the sources and the doubts.

  • 0.2People keep the initiative

    The Spacers let their robots do everything and stopped growing. Here machines propose and compute; people choose the questions and check the answers.

  • 0.3Credit, both ways

    Every entry says what the people did and what the machines did. On this site, gold marks the human strand and cyan the digital.

AlgorithmsDoug Colkitt + crowd

Integer multiplication below n log n

A public race of people and AI agents to multiply huge numbers in fewer than n log n steps.

n log n is the best speed proven so far for multiplying two n-digit numbers. κ (kappa) measures how far below it a method gets: the work grows like n·(log n)1−κ, so a bigger κ is faster. Conditional means it holds only if an unreviewed, machine-written paper is right.

Conditional · maintainer-reviewedNot peer reviewed

Founding member Doug Colkitt’s project (CrocSwap). Aska built the live race tracker that grew into this site, and submitted two small claims that were later superseded.

LOG₂ κ · DATED 23 SEP → NOWlog₂κ −9.22

Every reviewed record, from the AI-written paper’s 2⁻¹⁸² to today, on a halvings scale. All rest on that unreviewed paper.

Nutrition · offline app

Nutrak: a tracker that shows its work

Live demo · engine not publicNot clinically validated

A free, offline nutrition tracker with a plain-words “why this number?” receipt under every one of its 35 nutrient targets. It judges vitamins and minerals on a week of eating, never on one day.

RDA (1,000 mg): enough for about 97.5% of healthy adults. EAR (800 mg): enough for half. Judged day by day, Nutrak’s light for calcium would change 5 times in this demo week; judged on the 7-day average (777 mg), it gives one steady signal.
