06 // The writing

Look past the claim.

Ten dated essays from Aska Digital on what AI is actually changing, and on what has to be built around it for the change to last.

Most writing about AI argues about how clever the machines are. These essays ask a plainer question: what has to be true around a machine for its work to count? A proof counts only when someone can check it. A tool counts only when someone owns the outcome it is meant to improve. A public good lasts only when someone can pay for it without owning it.

  5. 10 Sep 2026Featured essay

    Traced argument

    Subtractive Fragility

02//One claim, drawn

From Math, faster · family 087

A shape times its shadow.

Math, faster rated one result the best-evidenced in OpenAI’s corpus: a proof of Mahler’s conjecture in every dimension. In two dimensions you can watch the whole idea.

Take a shape K centred on a point. Its polar, written K°, is a kind of shadow: the points y with x·y ≤ 1 for every point x of the shape (x·y, the dot product, multiplies matching coordinates and adds them). A big shape casts a small polar; a square casts a diamond, and a circle casts a circle. In 1939 Kurt Mahler asked how small the area of a shape times the area of its polar can be. The round shape gives the largest product. Mahler guessed that boxy shapes give the smallest, and proved that in the plane; in higher dimensions it has stayed largely open.

04//Claims with a date

Status as of 14 Oct 2026

Seven predictions, each with a date and a way to fail.

In Asimov’s Foundation, Hari Seldon’s recorded image appears in a Time Vault at moments he fixed in advance, each time to speak to the crisis he had predicted for that moment. Subtractive Fragility does the same in plain sight: seven claims, each with a scoring date and a stated condition under which it fails. If they fail, the essay says, its thesis is wrong.

05//How to read them

Arguments, not peer-reviewed research.

The house method

Each essay opens with its method, gives a section that states the strongest case against itself, closes with a compressed version of the argument, and lists its sources.

Who wrote them

The bylines are ASKA Research or The ASKA Team, at ASKA Business Solutions Inc. No post names an individual author, and none says, post by post, what AI did in the research or the writing.

Dated pieces

Market pieces carry their dates and say they are not investment or betting advice. Prices and counts in them are as reported on those dates, and many are labelled by the essays themselves as index-level figures that were not checked live.

Corrections
