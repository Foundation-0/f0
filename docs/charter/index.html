00 // The charter

The foundation that should have come first.

In Isaac Asimov’s stories, two Foundations were set up to shorten a dark age after the Galactic Empire fell. Foundation0 is our name for the step before them: people and machines working together in the open, so that no dark age comes.

00//The premise

Books and show kept apart

Asimov’s galaxy needed two Foundations. We think it needed a zeroth.

Asimov’s books

The Foundations

Isaac Asimov’s Foundation stories (magazine stories from 1942 to 1950, collected as three books from 1951 to 1953) open on a Galactic Empire that is quietly dying. The mathematician Hari Seldon has built psychohistory: statistics that predict what enormous populations will do, the way you can predict the pressure of a gas without following any one molecule. It tells him a dark age of about thirty thousand years is coming.116

He cannot stop the fall, but he can shorten what follows to about one thousand years. So he sets up two Foundations. The First, on Terminus, a planet at the galaxy’s rim, gathers a hundred thousand scientists under the cover story of compiling an encyclopedia. The Second, hidden, works on the science of minds and societies.27

Asimov’s books

Before them, the robots

Asimov’s robot stories come earlier in the same history, and by the time of the Empire there are almost no robots. The reason is not a war, a purge or a ban. People feared robots from the start; Asimov called that fear the Frankenstein complex. The first settlers of other worlds, the Spacers, lived among robots and slowly stopped growing. A second wave from Earth, the Settlers, spread through the galaxy without them.689

The robots that remained withdrew from sight. One of them, R. Daneel Olivaw, spent about twenty thousand years guiding humanity in secret, and was behind psychohistory itself. In Foundation and Earth (1986) he admits it: he has been steering people, for their own good, without their knowledge.1012

The Apple TV+ series

The show tells it differently

In the television series (2021 onward), robots and people fought the Robot Wars, and the robots lost. Demerzel, a robot who serves a dynasty of cloned emperors and secretly runs it, is one of the last survivors; in 2023 the show’s executive producer confirmed she is its version of Daneel. That war belongs to the show. It is not in Asimov’s books.1310

Our thesis

Every path failed in one of three ways

  • Fear. The Frankenstein complex in the books; open war in the show.
  • Dependence. The Spacers, who let their robots do everything.
  • Secrecy. Daneel’s hidden guidance, the hidden Second Foundation, a robot quietly running an emperor’s household.

Foundation0 is the organisation that should have come first: one that makes biological and digital intelligence work together in the open, so that the later Foundations are never needed. That is our claim, not Asimov’s. He never says that people and robots working well together would have prevented the fall.

Asimov’s books

Why zero

In Robots and Empire (1985), two robots, Daneel and Giskard, reason their way to a law that outranks the famous three, and Asimov numbered it zero, because a lower number comes first.95

And in the first Foundation story, in 1942, the colony on Terminus is called “Encyclopedia Foundation Number One”.1

Our thesis

Foundation0 is the number before one.

Not a rival to Seldon’s Foundations, and not a guardian: the groundwork that would have made them unnecessary. A public record of what people and machines discover together, research done the same way, and free tools for anyone to use.

No Robot War in the booksThe robot-free Empire in Asimov comes from a fade-out: the robot-bound Spacers decline, the Settlers expand without robots, and the robots that remain go into hiding. The Robot Wars and the purge belong to the Apple TV+ series. A “ban on thinking machines” belongs to a different saga altogether, Frank Herbert’s Dune.

01//Two futures

Two that went wrong, one we are building

The same story, told three ways.

Each path follows two strands, people in gold and machines in cyan, through the same five stages. The books and the show are fiction and are kept apart. Our path is real until it reaches the future, and every sentence on it is marked as a sourced fact or as our thesis.

02//Principles

Our thesis

Eight principles, each an answer to a way the old stories failed.

Fear grows where people cannot see or check what machines do. Dependence grows where machines do everything. Secrecy is guardianship without consent. Every principle below answers at least one of the three. They are ours, not Asimov’s, and they are not new laws of robotics.

Answers //

  1. 0.1

    We work in the open.

    Every guardian in Asimov’s galaxy, from the hidden Second Foundation to the robot Daneel, decided for people in secret, and no one could check them.

    Answers secrecy fear

  2. 0.2

    People choose the questions, and a named person decides and signs.

    The Spacers let their robots do everything and stopped growing; here machines propose and compute, and someone answers for every result.

    Answers dependence

  3. 0.3

    We credit both strands: what the people did and what the machines did.

    Hidden credit hides who is responsible; on this site gold marks the human strand and cyan the digital.

    Answers secrecy

  4. 0.4

    Every claim carries its status.

    A reader should see at a glance how sure anyone can be: claimed is never shown as verified, and a disputed result is marked as disputed.

    Answers fear secrecy

  5. 0.5

    Propose freely, verify strictly.

    Machines now write candidate proofs faster than anyone can check them, so checking is where the work, and the trust, now lives.

    Answers dependence fear

  6. 0.6

    We say how we could be wrong, and correct in public.

    Even the keepers of Seldon’s Plan taught that “the Seldon Plan is neither complete nor correct”; every result here lists its limits, and corrections stay in the history.2

    Answers fear

  7. 0.7

    Tools we build for the public good are free to use and show their working.

    A useful tool that only reaches those who can pay, or that hides how it decides, makes people depend on whoever owns it.

    Answers dependence

  8. 0.8

    No hidden guardian.

    Daneel steered humanity for twenty thousand years without asking; no person and no machine here may steer anyone in secret, however good the intention.

    Answers secrecy

04//Take part

Everything happens in public, on GitHub

Take part.

Foundation0 is a public record and a set of open projects. There are three ways in, and none needs permission.

  1. 01

    Propose a ledger entry

    Found an advance that AI caused or helped to cause? Draft it below: the form checks it against the Ledger’s rules and opens a GitHub issue that you can still edit before you submit it.

    Draft an entryThe rules (schema)

  2. 02

    Contribute to a project

    Every project page links its public repository. The integer-multiplication race takes results from anyone, people and their AI agents alike, on one condition: you don’t trust the sender, you run their checker. Its rules ask you to disclose your AI help.

    All projectsThe race’s repository

  3. 03

    Report an error

    If a number, a credit or a line of lore on this site is wrong, open an issue that names the page and the correction. Corrections are made in public, and the history is kept.

05//Sources

Where every line of lore comes from.

Scans of the original magazine printings for Asimov’s own words; standard references for his later novels and for the television series. Asimov revised his stories slightly for the books, so book wording can differ a little from the magazines.

  1. Astounding Science Fiction, May 1942: “Foundation” (later “The Encyclopedists”). Internet Archive scan: archive.org/details/Astounding_v29n03_1942-05. The hundred thousand scientists, the encyclopedia as a cover story, “Encyclopedia Foundation Number One”. ↩
  2. Astounding, November 1949: “…And Now You Don’t”, part 1. Scan: archive.org/details/Astounding_v44n03_1949-11_cape1736. Thirty thousand years cut to one thousand; the First Foundation’s physical science and the Second’s mental science; the Prime Radiant; “neither complete nor correct”. ↩
  3. Galaxy, October 1953: The Caves of Steel, part 1. Scan: archive.org/details/galaxymagazine-1953-10. C/Fe, “without priority”.
  4. Galaxy, November 1953: The Caves of Steel, part 2. Scan: archive.org/details/galaxymagazine-1953-11. The Spacer worlds will “degenerate and decay”.
  5. Wikipedia, Three Laws of Robotics: the laws first stated together in “Runaround” (1942); why the Zeroth Law is numbered zero; Asimov’s 1964 introduction to The Rest of the Robots.
  6. Wikipedia, Frankenstein complex: Asimov’s term for the fear of robots.
  7. Wikipedia, Foundation (novel series): publication history, the thirty-thousand-year summary, how the robot and Foundation series were joined.
  8. Wikipedia, Foundation universe: Aurora’s 200 million people and 10 billion robots; Daneel’s twenty thousand years.
  9. Wikipedia, Robots and Empire: the Settlers, Daneel and Giskard, the Zeroth Law.
  10. Wikipedia, R. Daneel Olivaw: his roles across the robot, Empire and Foundation books; David S. Goyer’s 2023 statements on the series.
  11. Wikipedia, Prelude to Foundation: Chetter Hummin and Eto Demerzel revealed as Daneel.
  12. Wikipedia, Foundation and Earth: Daneel’s admission that he has been steering humanity.
  13. Wikipedia, Foundation (TV series): Demerzel, the Robot Wars, the cloned emperors, the Foundation in exile; seasons and renewal.
  14. Apple TV+ press, Foundation: the series’ own summary.
  15. Wikiquote, Isaac Asimov: the Zeroth Law’s wording in Robots and Empire.
  16. The Encyclopedia of Science Fiction, psychohistory: statistics of large populations who do not know they are being predicted.
