00//How to read it

Read this first

Six things to know before you read the record.

Every entry is one record in a public file, ledger.json, and follows the same schema: a list of the fields each entry must have, and what each may hold.

0.1

What counts as an advance

A result that became public and that someone else can check: a proof, a method measured against the best one before it, a discovery, or a system put into daily use. It enters only if AI did part of the work, and only with a source anyone can open.

Each entry gives the date the result became public and says how: a journal paper, a preprint, an announcement, a competition or a deployment. Significance, from 1 to 5, is our editorial judgement of how much it changed its field, and is labelled as such.

0.2

What did the AI do?

  • Instrument 86

    A tool inside a study people designed.

    e.g. GraphCast forecasts the weather (2023)

  • Collaborator 96

    Proposes key ideas; people select, prove or test them.

    e.g. a model points to the antibiotic halicin (2020)

  • Originator 61

    Produces the result itself; people check it.

    e.g. AlphaGo’s move 37 (2016)

The dial on each mark is the machine’s share in cyan, the people’s in gold. It never fills completely: people still check the result.

0.3

How sure is it?

  1. Formally verifieda proof checker, a program such as Lean, has checked every step26
  2. Replicatedsomeone outside the team confirmed it independently12
  3. Peer reviewedexperts checked it for a journal or a conference145
  4. Deployedin daily use, where failure would show17
  5. Preprintwritten up in full and public, not yet reviewed29
  6. Claimedannounced; the evidence is not yet public or not yet checked14

The glyph counts rungs, one to five. A claim is never shown as verified.

0.4

× Contested

A credible person or group has disputed the result in public: how strong it is, whether it is new, whether the comparison was fair, or who deserves the credit. We keep the entry, mark it with ×, and quote the dispute in a note.

24 entries are contested. Many others carry a caveat that is not a dispute, such as “no human referee yet”.

0.5

The red hairline

In Asimov’s Second Foundation the Prime Radiant shows Seldon’s equations in black on pearl-white walls; every red mark is a later contribution. Here a red hairline marks Foundation0’s own: work by its founding members, or work Aska Digital contributed to.

Each such entry says exactly who did what. There are 10; the “Members’ work” filter shows only them.

04//Keeping the record

Open to additions and corrections

How an entry gets in, and how to add one.

  1. Find a result and a source

    Something that became public, with a link anyone can open: a paper, a preprint, a repository, an official announcement. AI must have done part of the work.

  2. Write it as one entry

    Plain words, a summary of at most 60 words, what it changed (before and after), the AI systems and the people or organisations involved, and the sources.

  3. Label it honestly

    One AI role, one rung of evidence, contested or not, with a note saying why, and a significance from 1 to 5.

  4. Let the gate check it

    Before anything is published, scripts/check_site.py checks every entry against the schema: required fields, lengths, dates that are not in the future, secure source links, a note for every contested entry, and date order.

Propose an entry → Edit ledger.json on GitHub

An issue needs only the result and one source. A pull request adds the entry to docs/data/ledger.json itself, in date order. Spotted an error? Report it the same way.

Every field, in plain words

id
A short permanent name; also this page’s link to the entry, as in ledger/#alphago-move-37.
date, date_basis
When the result became public, and how: publication, preprint, announcement, competition or deployment.
domain, subfield
The field (one of eleven) and a finer label.
summary
What happened, in at most 60 words.
before_after
The measure that moved, its value before and after. Left empty when there is no single measure.
why_it_matters
One or two sentences, at most 40 words.
ai_systems, organizations
Which programs did the machine’s part, and who the people were.
ai_role
Instrument, collaborator or originator.
evidence
Formally verified, independently replicated, peer reviewed, deployed, preprint or claimed.
contested, contested_note
Whether the result is publicly disputed, and the note that says by whom and why. The note also holds caveats.
significance
1 to 5, an editorial judgement.
sources
One to four links, all https.
foundation0, page
True for founding members’ work or work Aska Digital contributed to, and the Foundation0 page about it, if there is one.
